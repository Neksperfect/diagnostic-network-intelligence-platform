# DNIP Data Quality and Validation

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the data quality controls and validation checks
for the Diagnostic Network Intelligence Platform (DNIP). It describes
how the project will assess whether synthetic records are complete,
valid, consistent, relationally sound, and financially reconcilable.

The checks apply to data generation, loading, database operations,
analytical preparation, and reporting. They complement PostgreSQL
primary keys, foreign keys, unique constraints, CHECK constraints, and
triggers.

## 2. Scope and data status

DNIP models the fictional Meridian Diagnostics Network (MDN). All
records are synthetic and must be clearly identified as such in project
documentation and reporting.

The main analytical period is **1 November 2022 to 31 October 2026**.
Development and concurrency-test records outside this period must be
excluded from the main business dataset using documented filters. They
should not be deleted merely because they are outside the reporting
period.

The current database has four schemas:

-   `staging`: reserved for future data landing and preparation.
-   `core`: operational relational tables and financial balance views.
-   `mart`: reserved for future analytical tables and views.
-   `audit`: ETL run and data-quality issue tracking.

The core schema currently contains 30 relational tables and two
financial balance views. The audit schema contains `etl_runs` and
`data_quality_issues`.

## 3. Data quality dimensions

DNIP validation is organised around these dimensions:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Dimension                           What is checked
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Completeness                        Required fields and expected
                                      records are present

  Uniqueness                          Business identifiers and records
                                      are not duplicated

  Validity                            Values comply with permitted
                                      formats, ranges, and status domains

  Referential integrity               Foreign keys reference existing
                                      parent records

  Consistency                         Related fields and records agree
                                      with one another

  Timeliness                          Dates and timestamps follow the
                                      expected reporting period and
                                      sequence

  Financial accuracy                  Invoice, payment, refund,
                                      settlement, and revenue amounts
                                      reconcile
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

A failed check should be investigated and documented. Do not silently
modify records just to make a validation query pass.

## 4. Structural and referential integrity

### 4.1 Primary keys and identity columns

Every record must have a valid primary key. Identity columns are
generated by PostgreSQL and must not be manually assigned by
data-generation scripts.

Important identity columns include:

-   `customer_id`
-   `payer_id`
-   `invoice_id`
-   `payment_id`
-   `allocation_id`
-   `refund_id`
-   `reversal_id`
-   `adjustment_id`
-   `settlement_batch_id`
-   `settlement_line_id`

Dependent records should use identifiers returned by PostgreSQL or
safely looked up through stable business keys.

### 4.2 Foreign keys

Child records must reference valid parent records. Validation should
cover, among other relationships:

-   Collection centres, staff, equipment, and expenses to branches.
-   Specimens to patients, collection centres, and specimen types.
-   Test instances to specimens, tests, staff, and equipment where
    applicable.
-   Invoice lines to invoices and test instances.
-   Invoices to customers and payers.
-   Payment allocations to payments and invoices.
-   Refunds to payments.
-   Settlement lines to settlement batches and payments.
-   Inventory lots to reagents.
-   Inventory movements to lots and branches.

PostgreSQL foreign keys provide the primary enforcement mechanism.
Additional anti-join checks can be used during staging and ETL
validation to identify orphaned records before loading.

### 4.3 Uniqueness

The database has unique constraints on business identifiers, including
codes for branches, collection centres, customers, equipment, expenses,
invoices, patients, payers, refunds, payments, reagents, settlement
batches, staff, test instances, and tests.

Additional unique constraints apply to selected reference names and
mappings. In particular, `invoice_lines.test_instance_id` is unique, so
one test instance cannot be assigned to multiple invoice lines.

Generation and ETL scripts must use stable business keys and must be
safe to rerun without creating duplicate business records.

## 5. Domain and status validation

Generated values must comply with the database's permitted values and
CHECK constraints.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Entity                              Validation rules
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Customers                           `customer_type` is `Individual` or
                                      `Corporate`

  Payers                              `payer_type` is `Individual`,
                                      `Corporate`, or `Insurance`; credit
                                      terms are zero or greater

  Patients                            Sex is `Female`, `Male`, `Unknown`,
                                      or `NULL`

  Staff                               Status is `Active`, `On Leave`,
                                      `Resigned`, or `Terminated`

  Equipment                           Status is `Operational`,
                                      `Under Maintenance`,
                                      `Out of Service`, or `Retired`

  Specimens                           Status is `Registered`, `Received`,
                                      `In Processing`, `Completed`,
                                      `Rejected`, or `Cancelled`

  Test instances                      Status is `Ordered`, `In Progress`,
                                      `Completed`, `Validated`, `Failed`,
                                      or `Cancelled`

  QC events                           Status is `Pass`, `Fail`, or
                                      `Warning`

  Expenses                            Payment status is `Paid`, `Unpaid`,
                                      or `Partially Paid`; amount is
                                      positive

  Inventory movements                 Movement type is one of the
                                      permitted movement types; quantity
                                      is positive

  Invoices                            Status is `Draft`, `Issued`,
                                      `Partially Paid`, `Paid`,
                                      `Overdue`, or `Cancelled`

  Payments                            Method and status must use the
                                      permitted values defined in the
                                      core schema; amount is positive

  Refunds                             Status is `Pending`, `Successful`,
                                      `Failed`, or `Cancelled`; amount is
                                      positive

  Settlement batches                  Status is `Pending`, `Settled`, or
                                      `Failed`

  Adjustments                         Type is `Credit Note`, `Write-off`,
                                      or `Debit Note`; approval is
                                      `Pending`, `Approved`, or
                                      `Rejected`

  Receivables impairments             Status is `Active` or `Reversed`;
                                      provision amount is positive

  Revenue recognition                 Event type is `Recognition` or
                                      `Reversal`, with reversal
                                      references and reasons required for
                                      reversal events
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Payment methods permitted by the schema are `Bank Transfer`, `POS`,
`Card`, `Cash`, and `Online Payment`. Payment statuses are `Successful`,
`Pending`, `Failed`, `Reversed`, and `Refunded`.

Inventory movement types are `Receipt`, `Issue`, `Adjustment In`,
`Adjustment Out`, `Transfer In`, `Transfer Out`, `Expired`, `Return In`,
and `Return Out`.

## 6. Date and timestamp validation

Related dates and timestamps must follow valid business sequences.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Record or process                   Required validation
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Specimen                            `collected_at` must not be later
                                      than `received_at`

  Test instance                       `processing_started_at` must be at
                                      or after `ordered_at`, when
                                      supplied

  Test instance                       `completed_at` must be at or after
                                      `ordered_at`, when supplied

  Validated test                      Completion timestamp must exist;
                                      `result_validated_at` must be at or
                                      after `completed_at`

  Repeat test                         A repeat must reference an existing
                                      original test instance and cannot
                                      reference itself

  Invoice                             `due_date` must be on or after
                                      `invoice_date`

  Inventory lot                       `expiry_date` must be on or after
                                      `received_date`

  Equipment downtime                  `resolved_at` is required for
                                      `Resolved` status and cannot
                                      precede `started_at`

  Reporting period                    Main analytical records must fall
                                      within 1 November 2022 to 31
                                      October 2026 unless explicitly
                                      treated as an opening balance or
                                      documented exception
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Specimen rejection flags and rejection details must agree. For example,
a rejected specimen should have a consistent rejection status and
supporting rejection details according to the table constraints.

## 7. Financial validation

Financial validation is a critical part of DNIP. Cash received,
recognised revenue, VAT, and receivables are different measures and must
not be combined.

### 7.1 Invoice and invoice-line reconciliation

For each invoice line:

-   Quantity must be greater than zero.
-   Unit price must be zero or greater.
-   Discount must be zero or greater and cannot exceed quantity
    multiplied by unit price.
-   VAT rate must be zero or greater.
-   Line subtotal and VAT amount must be zero or greater.
-   `line_total` must equal `line_subtotal + vat_amount`.

At invoice level:

-   Invoice subtotal must equal the sum of its invoice-line subtotals.
-   Invoice VAT must equal the sum of its invoice-line VAT amounts.
-   Invoice total must equal subtotal plus VAT.

The project must also validate the financial balance view
`core.vw_invoice_financial_balances`, including adjusted invoice amount,
gross and net allocations, and outstanding balance. Approved adjustments
affect the receivable calculation; pending and rejected adjustments must
not be treated as approved.

### 7.2 Payment and allocation reconciliation

Payment transactions must have positive amounts. Allocations must have
positive amounts and must reference valid payment and invoice records.

Validation must confirm:

-   The payment and invoice have the same payer.
-   Only eligible successful payments are allocated.
-   Allocations do not exceed the available payment amount.
-   Net allocations do not exceed the invoice's adjusted outstanding
    balance.
-   Allocation corrections are represented by reversal records rather
    than edits or deletions of historical allocations.

Use `core.vw_payment_financial_balances` to review original payment
amount, successful refunds, net payment retained, gross and net
allocations, and unapplied balance.

### 7.3 Refund controls

Refund amounts must be positive and must reference valid payments.

Pending and successful refunds reserve payment funds. Failed and
cancelled refunds release the reservation.

Validation must confirm that combined refund reservations and pending
settlement reservations do not exceed the original payment amount.
Successful refunds must also respect the remaining amount available
after allocations and other applicable reservations.

A payment must not be marked `Refunded` unless the project's full-refund
conditions are met, including successful refunds covering the full
payment, no pending refunds, no net allocations, and no pending
settlement batch.

### 7.4 Settlement controls

Settlement lines must satisfy:

-   Gross settlement amount is greater than zero.
-   Processing fee is zero or greater.
-   Net settlement amount is zero or greater.
-   Net settlement amount equals gross amount less processing fee.
-   The payment method is eligible for processor settlement.
-   Cumulative settlement gross does not exceed the original payment
    amount.

Pending settlement lines reserve funds. Failed batches release
reservations. Settled batches are historical completed settlements and
must be retained for auditability.

Direct cash and bank-transfer reconciliation should not be treated as
processor settlement unless a specific workflow is implemented for them.

### 7.5 Revenue recognition and adjustments

Revenue recognition is linked to invoice lines. Recognition should
follow the project's service-completion policy, with the diagnostic
service recognised when the test is completed and validated, not simply
when cash is received.

Validation must confirm:

-   Recognition amounts are positive.
-   Recognition events do not carry a reversal reference.
-   Reversal events reference an original recognition event and include
    a reason.
-   Reversal amounts and original events are consistent with the
    intended reversal logic.
-   VAT is not included as service revenue.
-   Advance payments remain unearned until the related service is
    completed.

Only approved invoice adjustments affect receivable balances. Credit
notes, write-offs, debit notes, and receivables impairment provisions
must remain distinct.

## 8. Laboratory, quality, equipment, and inventory validation

### 8.1 Test and specimen workflow

Validate that every test instance references a valid specimen and test.
Where supplied, staff and equipment references must also exist.

Check that test timestamps and status values agree. A validated test
must have a completion timestamp. Repeat-test flags and references must
be consistent.

### 8.2 Quality control

QC events must use valid statuses. Where both expected minimum and
maximum values are supplied, the minimum must not exceed the maximum.
Optional equipment, staff, and test references must resolve to valid
records.

QC failures and warnings should be available for operational review.
They must not be silently removed from reporting datasets.

### 8.3 Equipment downtime

Downtime records must reference valid equipment. Status must be `Open`,
`Under Repair`, or `Resolved`. A resolved event must have a resolution
timestamp, and the timestamp must not precede the start time.

Operational reporting should distinguish equipment that is under
maintenance, out of service, or retired from equipment that is
operational.

### 8.4 Inventory

Inventory lots must reference valid reagents. Expiry dates cannot
precede receipt dates. Unit cost, when present, must be zero or greater.

Inventory movements must have positive quantities and valid movement
types. Where supplied, branch, staff, and test-instance references must
be valid.

Operational analysis should distinguish receipts, issues, transfers,
adjustments, returns, and expired stock. Do not assume every movement
type has the same effect on available stock.

## 9. Validation execution approach

Validation should occur at several points:

1.  **Before loading:** Check generated files for missing required
    values, duplicate business keys, invalid domains, and invalid dates.
2.  **During loading:** Let PostgreSQL constraints and triggers enforce
    structural and transactional rules.
3.  **After loading:** Run SQL checks for expected counts, orphaned
    references, duplicates, date sequences, and financial
    reconciliation.
4.  **Before reporting:** Confirm reporting-period filters, exclude
    documented development test records, and reconcile dashboard
    measures to the core financial views.
5.  **After changes:** Rerun the relevant validation checks whenever
    generation logic, triggers, financial rules, or reporting logic
    changes.

Validation SQL should be stored in `sql/03_data_validation/`. Checks
should have clear names and should report the failing records or
exception counts, not only a generic pass/fail message.

## 10. Issue recording and remediation

Use `audit.data_quality_issues` to record data-quality exceptions where
its actual column design supports the required details. Use
`audit.etl_runs` to record ETL execution outcomes and useful row counts
or error details supported by its table design.

Before writing audit insert statements, inspect the actual columns and
required fields in PostgreSQL. Do not assume column names or insert
values based on this document alone.

Each issue should be investigated, classified, and resolved through a
controlled correction or documented exception. Preserve the original
evidence where auditability matters. Do not disable constraints or
triggers simply to force a load to succeed.

## 11. Reporting and acceptance criteria

A dataset is ready for analytical reporting when:

-   Required records have been loaded and expected counts have been
    reconciled.
-   Primary keys and foreign keys are valid.
-   No unexplained duplicate business identifiers remain.
-   Required fields and permitted values pass validation.
-   Date and timestamp sequences are valid.
-   Invoice totals reconcile to invoice lines.
-   Payment allocations, refunds, and settlement reservations comply
    with the financial rules.
-   Revenue recognition and reversal records are internally consistent.
-   Laboratory, QC, equipment, and inventory records pass their
    applicable checks.
-   Development-only records are excluded from main reporting through
    documented rules.
-   Dashboards and reports disclose that the data is synthetic.

Any unresolved exception that could materially change a KPI or financial
result must be documented and excluded from the affected reporting
measure until resolved.

## 12. Implementation status

This document defines the validation framework. It does not mean that
all validation SQL has already been written or executed.

The database currently has the core relational schema, financial balance
views, constraints, and validation triggers described in the project
documentation. The next implementation step is to create individual SQL
validation scripts, execute them against generated data, record the
results, and resolve any exceptions before building the analytical marts
and dashboards.
